Hi, I'm Stephen Agyeah, a PhD student in Data Science and Analytics at North Carolina A&T State University, where I also earned my Master's in Applied Mathematics with a concentration in Statistics and Data Science.

I currently work as a Graduate Research Assistant on an NSF funded project studying how students and faculty interact with AI tools in data science education. My work includes statistical analysis of survey data, analyzing student and AI interaction patterns, developing course materials and evaluation rubrics, and building an AI agent using Microsoft Copilot to automate parts of the grading process. I also serve as a Graduate Teaching Assistant, supporting undergraduate students in statistics and statistical programming using R.

Before graduate school, I worked as a Data Analyst at Ghana Commercial Bank, using R, SQL, and Python to analyze data, generate insights, and improve operational performance.

My research interests include machine learning, predictive modeling, simulation, explainable AI, and statistical methodology, with applications in health, finance, and education. I am particularly interested in statistical methods for sensitive data collection, including distribution function estimation using the Item Sum Technique (IST) and Item Sum Double List Technique (ISDLT). I am also involved in early stage collaborative research exploring the use of list experiments to estimate the prevalence of labor trafficking in Malawi and Zambia, where underreporting and social desirability bias present significant challenges.

I am especially drawn to research that combines strong statistical and computational methods with real world impact, producing results that are not only technically sound but also interpretable, actionable, and useful for decision making.
