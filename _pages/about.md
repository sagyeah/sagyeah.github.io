I was born and raised in Ghana, where I developed a love for problem solving early on, whether it was breaking down math problems or trying to make sense of everyday challenges through numbers. That curiosity led me to study Mathematics at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST), where I earned my BSc in Mathematics. Those years built the analytical foundation I now bring to research, data, and real world problems.

I earned my Master’s degree in Applied Mathematics with a concentration in Statistics and Data Science from North Carolina A&T State University (NCA&T), graduating with a 3.93 GPA. I am now pursuing a PhD in Data Science and Analytics at NCA&T, where I continue to deepen my interests in statistical methodology, machine learning, simulation, predictive modeling, and explainable AI.

Before graduate school, I worked as a Data Analyst at Ghana Commercial Bank PLC in Accra, where I experienced firsthand how data can influence real financial decisions. Using R, SQL, and Python, I analyzed operational data, generated actionable insights, and contributed to projects that increased processing efficiency by 15% and helped reduce account closures by 21% through sentiment analysis.

At NCA&T, I work as a Graduate Research Assistant on an NSF funded project exploring how students and faculty interact with AI tools in data science education. My work has included statistical analysis of survey data, simulation studies, analyzing student and AI interaction patterns, developing course materials and evaluation rubrics, and building an AI agent using Microsoft Copilot to automate parts of the grading process. I also serve as a Graduate Teaching Assistant, guiding undergraduate students through statistics and statistical programming in R.

My research also extends to statistical methods for collecting and analyzing sensitive information. In June 2025, I presented my research on Distribution Function Estimation for Sensitive Quantitative Variables at the NISS New Researchers Network Virtual Conference. My interests include indirect questioning methods such as the Item Sum Technique and Item Sum Double List Technique, and I am currently involved in early stage collaborative research exploring the use of list experiments to estimate the prevalence of labor trafficking in Malawi and Zambia.

What drives me is the opportunity to use rigorous statistical and computational methods to solve meaningful problems. I am particularly interested in applications across health, finance, and education, and in research that produces results that are interpretable, actionable, and useful for decision making. I am always interested in research collaborations, data science opportunities, and projects where strong statistical thinking can create measurable impact.

Outside of research and teaching, I enjoy football, playing games, and music. They remind me that creativity, discipline, curiosity, and human connection matter just as much as technical skill.
