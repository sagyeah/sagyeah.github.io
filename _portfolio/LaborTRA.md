### Overview

This research, currently being developed into a **manuscript for publication**, investigates the effectiveness of indirect survey methods for estimating the prevalence of labor trafficking, a severely underreported form of exploitation where conventional direct questioning may be affected by social desirability bias and underreporting.

The study focuses on the **List Experiment**, also known as the **Item Count Technique**, as an indirect questioning method designed to elicit more truthful responses to sensitive questions while protecting respondent anonymity. The empirical analysis uses real world data collected in **Malawi and Zambia**, where measuring hidden forms of labor exploitation through conventional survey approaches presents significant challenges.

### Methodology

The study empirically evaluates the use of list experiments for estimating the prevalence of hidden forms of labor exploitation in resource constrained, real world settings. The analysis examines the practical performance and limitations of the method, the conditions under which it succeeds or encounters challenges, and potential refinements for studying highly sensitive and underreported experiences.

List experiment estimates are compared with direct survey estimates to examine differences in reported prevalence. The analysis also incorporates **Prevalence Reduction Innovation Forum (PRIF)** indicators to support the evaluation and interpretation of prevalence estimates across the study populations.

### Research Context

This ongoing research is a collaboration between researchers at **North Carolina A&T State University** and the **University of Georgia**, involving **Dr. Hui Yi, Dr. Sayed Mostafa, Dr. Laura Zimmerman, Dr. David Okech**, and other members of the research team.

The project connects directly to my broader research interests in **statistical methodology for sensitive data collection, indirect questioning techniques, and methods for addressing underreporting in vulnerable populations**. Through this work, I am expanding my experience in applying statistical methodology to complex real world problems where conventional data collection approaches may not adequately capture the true prevalence of sensitive behaviors or experiences.
