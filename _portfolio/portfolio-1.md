### Overview

This research, currently being developed into a **manuscript for publication**, investigates statistical methods for estimating the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of sensitive quantitative variables, where direct questioning may produce inaccurate responses due to social desirability bias or privacy concerns.

The study focuses on two indirect questioning methods, the **Item Sum Technique (IST)** and the **Item Sum Double List Technique (ISDLT)**, which are designed to protect respondent privacy while enabling population level estimation.

### Methodology

Using Monte Carlo simulation in R, the study evaluates CDF estimators across different sample sizes, correlation structures, and distributional assumptions. Social desirability bias is incorporated into the simulation framework to examine how underreporting affects direct estimates and how indirect questioning methods can improve estimation.

The analysis compares estimator performance using measures including bias, mean squared error (MSE), and visual agreement with the true distribution. The framework also extends to quantile estimation and real data applications.

### Contributions

- Developed and implemented Monte Carlo simulation workflows in R
- Implemented and evaluated CDF estimators based on IST and ISDLT
- Compared estimator performance across sample sizes and correlation structures
- Evaluated estimator bias, MSE, and distributional fit
- Extended the analysis to quantile estimation and real data applications
- Contributed to research coding, statistical analysis, and manuscript development
